// MODEL
\subsection{DistilBERT}
DistilBERT is a lightweight, pre-trained transformer model that is derived from BERT (Bidirectional Representations from Transformers). DistilBERT is a derivative of the BERT model as it retains most of its language understanding capabilities while being lighting and faster. This is achieved by DistilBERT by using a technique called knowledge distillation, where a smaller "child" model is trained to replicate the performance of the "parent" model \cite{sanh2019distilbert, devlin2019bert}. 

DistilBERT follows the conventional transformer encoder design \cite{vaswani2017attention}; however, it has a recued depth of 6 layers (compared to BERT's 12). Each layer consists of multi-head self-attention and position-wise feed-forward networks, allowing the model to generalize contextualized token representation. The self-attention mechanism enables each token to pay attention to all other tokens in the sequence, capturing long-range depensencies and context critical for sentiment understanding. 

Input text is first processed with the use of a tokenizer that implements subword tokenizer and maps tokens into embeddings. These embeddings combine token and positional information, which are then spread across through the layers. For classification, a task-specific linear head is added on top of the final hidden state, creating logits for binary sentiment prediction.

A key advantage of DistilBERT is its capability to encode bidirectional context, allowing it to capture complex patterns such as negation and sentiment. Minimal processing is required as the tokenizer and pre-trained embeddings are optimized for raw text inputs. 

Overall, DistilBERT offers an efficient trade-off between computational cost and performance, making it well-suited for fine-tuned tasks such as sentiment classification.


// RESULTS
\subsection{DistilBERT}
DistilBERT achieves an accuracy of 88.46% and an F1-score of 0.875, showing strong overall performance on the task of binary sentiment classification. The small gap between the accuracy and F1-score implies that the model maintains a balanced performance between both classes, without any bias on either positive or negative predictions.

The performance of the model can be credited to its use of contextual embeddings, allowing it to capture semantic and syntactic relationships beyond simple word frequencies. Compared to traditional approaches used in prior projects such as TF-IDF-based models, DistilBERT adapts deep bidirectional representations that improves its ability to comprehend nuanced linguistic occurences such as negation and subtle cues of sentiment.

Moreover, the use of a train-test split ensures that the class distribution remains consistent throughout the dataset. The results indicate that the model generalizes fairly well to unseen data with no signs of overfitting.
